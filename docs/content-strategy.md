# Content strategy

## Positioning

tinylion1024 is a minimalist AI product builder.

The working sentence is:

> Build products with AI, validate judgment through real growth, and remove low-value complexity.

Every page and article should strengthen at least one part of that sentence. The site is not a broad technology-news feed, a tool-review directory, or a personal résumé.

## Four editorial tracks

| Track | Core question | Typical evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| AI Product | What becomes a better product because AI exists? | Product decisions, model economics, user behavior |
| AI Building | How can AI shorten delivery without weakening judgment? | Workflows, code, tests, failure cases |
| Growth Validation | Did the product create repeated value? | Experiments, retention, activation, distribution |
| Personal Thinking | What complexity can be removed? | Principles, decisions, considered trade-offs |

Suggested long-term mix:

- 35% AI Building
- 30% AI Product
- 20% Growth Validation
- 15% Personal Thinking

This is a directional balance, not a publishing quota.

## Representative beliefs

These are the ideas the archive should keep testing, refining, or challenging:

1. A product is a set of validated trade-offs, not a feature list.
2. Models are infrastructure; the delivery system determines whether AI creates value.
3. Growth is the result of value being repeatedly used and shared, not a traffic trick.
4. Minimalism is the practice of removing low-value complexity, not a visual style.
5. Process should scale with risk. Small reversible work should stay light.

## Publishing cadence

The default pace is intentionally low:

- Two deep, bilingual essays per month.
- One `/now` update whenever the working focus materially changes.
- One project note or measured experiment per month once real data exists.

Do not publish to fill a calendar. A draft waits until it contains a clear claim, original judgment, and evidence that a reader can use.

## Editorial bar

Before a piece enters the site, it should answer:

1. What is the one-sentence claim?
2. What did I observe, build, measure, or decide that supports it?
3. What useful choice can a reader make differently afterward?
4. Which part is fact, which part is inference, and which part is personal judgment?
5. Is the English version adapted for its reader rather than translated literally?

Time-sensitive claims require a date and direct sources. Tool articles require a real selection principle, not a feature-list comparison. AI workflow articles require a failure mode, a boundary, and a verification method.

## Information architecture thresholds

- Homepage: three Start Here essays and the four latest essays.
- Series navigation: enabled when at least two articles share a series.
- Dedicated series landing pages: add when a series reaches four substantial articles.
- Search: add when the archive reaches roughly 24 substantial articles or readers report discovery friction.
- Tags: do not add a tag cloud. Keep the four editorial tracks as the primary taxonomy.

## Success signals

Prefer signals of trust and usefulness over raw page views:

- Direct replies and thoughtful email conversations
- Returning readers and RSS subscribers
- Essays cited, saved, or shared with commentary
- Product or collaboration conversations caused by a specific essay
- Readers moving from an essay to About, Projects, or another piece in the same series

Analytics stays optional and privacy-friendly. No tracking pixel is required to decide whether the writing is useful.
